# DQ903 identity-break

**Family:** statements, integrity and identities (DQ90x) · **Severity:** warn by
default, from `p_error`; info for a filing fact · **Applies to:** statements ·
**Bayesian** · **Needs:** `[statements.identities]`

## What it reports

A configured identity that fails on a version beyond its tolerance, and why
([statements.md](../statements.md#identities-and-ties)). Every version is judged,
as fafnir judges every current one. An identity whose item the series lacks, or
whose term is NULL on the row, is not judged there, except that an item in
`[statements] null_is_zero` is read as 0 where NULL (fafnir's prior names
`fx_effect_on_cash`, as its `cash_flow_tie` does).

**Tolerance.** An identity holds within the larger of `identity_share` (0.5%) of
its largest term and `tie_floor` (1M of the reporting currency): fafnir's
tolerance for `balance_sheet_identity` and `cash_flow_tie`.

**Why.** A gap beyond it has four explanations, weighed by the evidence:

| Hypothesis | Evidence for it | Classification, severity |
|---|---|---|
| `structural` | the same gap in the neighbouring periods in force (two either side, of the period type), within the larger of the tolerance and a quarter of the gap, by their median or in both periods on one side (a gap that starts or stops here: a SPAC's first quarters); the identity failing in every neighbouring period by other amounts ("a gap in every period is the company's structure"); or an `explained_by` item reported, non-zero, in the period | market fact, info |
| `reclassification` | another version of the period holds, with the same total (the left side) | market fact, info |
| `field_error` | the gap is a stored term put wrong: dropped from the total or counted twice (the term), its sign flipped (twice the term), or stored in thousands or millions where units were meant (999 or 999,999 times the term), or the other way. The match must be within a fifth of the tolerance, and the value put right must sit with the same item in the neighbouring periods better than the stored one does | data error, from `p_error` |
| `unexplained` | none of the above | data error, from `p_error` |

`p_error` is the probability of the last two. The prior weights and Bayes factors
are dorq's defaults until DR-0911 fits them to fafnir's labels. A field error
names the field: in the message, in `detail.field`, in `record_key` as `item`,
and in a `check_item` suggested action. Where a total wrong and a term dropped
from it both fit, the total is named, since it is wrong in either reading.

```
AAA  2010-09-30 Q3 FY2010 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 error  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 5994000000 (tolerance 50000000): the gap is total_liabilities stored at x0.001  (line 8)
BBB  2010-03-31 Q1 FY2010 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 info  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 300000000 (tolerance 50000000): as in the periods around it  (line 3)
CCC  2010-09-30 Q3 FY2010 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 info  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 250000000 (tolerance 50000000): as in the periods around it, with net_income_noncontrolling reported  (line 10)
```

The other messages are `as in the periods before it` (or `after it`), `it fails
in the periods around it too`, `net_income_noncontrolling is reported in the
period`, `another version holds, with the same total_assets`, and `no term
explains it`. "Stored at x0.001" is a value a thousand times too small;
"twice X: its sign" is X with its sign flipped.

**Keys.** A report's `record_key` is the version's, with `identity`, and for a
field error also `item`:
`{"statement":"balance_sheet","period_type":"quarter","fiscal_date":"2010-09-30","version_no":1,"identity":"balance_sheet","item":"total_liabilities"}`.
Its detail holds `identity`, `equation`, `gap` (left side minus right),
`tolerance`, `neighbours_gap` (the median of the neighbours' gaps, where there are
two or more) and `field`. The hypotheses and evidence are in the JSON formats'
`hypotheses` and `evidence`.

**fafnir.** DQ903 succeeds `balance_sheet_identity` and `cash_flow_tie`, and
judges the subtotals they do not: current and noncurrent, gross profit, operating
income, EBIT, and the net income chain (`priors/fafnir-statements.toml`). It
does what fafnir's playbook does by hand ("a gap in every period is the company's
structure ... a gap in one period alone, unexplained by the filing, is a vendor
error"). Two things differ:

- fafnir's balance-sheet check calls a negative gap an error and never
  structural. DQ903 has no sign rule: a gap in every period is structural
  either way.
- fafnir flags every gap at one severity. DQ903 reports a filing fact at info,
  which fafnir's ingest files without a queue entry.

**SF1's identities.** The prior has the candidates of the plan's §4.2 that SF1
does not compute. `scripts/fafnir-identity-pass-rates.sql` measures each one's
pass rate on fafnir's current versions, with DQ903's tolerance. On a sample of
400 random SF1 tickers (2026-11-01, as reported and as restated):

| Identity | Pass rate | Shipped |
|---|---|---|
| `gross_profit`, `operating_income`, `ebit` | 100% | no: SF1 computes them |
| `assets_split` | 100% as reported, 99.92% restated | yes |
| `liabilities_split`, `debt_split`, `investments_split` | 99.86% to 99.995% | yes |
| `net_income`, `net_income_common` | 99.62% to 99.94% | yes |
| `cash_flow` | 96.9% to 97.0% | yes, investigated: DQ903 files 46% of its failures as structural or reclassifications |
| `balance_sheet` | 83.7% to 83.9% | yes, investigated: DQ903 files 92% of its failures as structural or reclassifications |

Two of fafnir's exemptions are balance-sheet rules DQ903 does not yet have, and
are most of what it still reports there at error: temporary equity (the parent's
equity a deficit and the gap at least 20% of assets, over too few periods to
persist: a SPAC's first year) and minority interest before SFAS 160 (a positive
gap up to 20% of assets, before 2009-12-15). DR-0912's shadow comparison
measures them against fafnir's SQL check before dorq replaces it.

## Fix

A field error: re-fetch the version, and if the vendor's value is the one named,
report it to the vendor. A structural gap: nothing to fix; add the item that
explains it to `explained_by` where the vendor reports one. A reclassification:
the newer version is the one in force. Unexplained: compare the version with the
filing.

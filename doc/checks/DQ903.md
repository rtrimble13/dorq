# DQ903 identity-break

**Family:** statements, integrity and identities (DQ90x) · **Severity:** warn by
default, from `p_error`; info for a filing fact · **Applies to:** statements ·
**Bayesian** · **Needs:** `[statements.identities]`

## What it reports

A configured identity that fails on a version beyond its tolerance, and why
([statements.md](../statements.md#identities-and-ties)). Every version is judged,
as fafnir judges every current one. An identity whose item the series lacks, or
whose term is NULL on the row, is not judged there, except that an item in
`[statements] null_is_zero` is read as 0 where NULL (fafnir's prior names
`fx_effect_on_cash`, as its `cash_flow_tie` does).

**Tolerance.** An identity holds within the larger of `identity_share` (0.5%) of
its largest term and `tie_floor` (1M of the reporting currency): fafnir's
tolerance for `balance_sheet_identity` and `cash_flow_tie`.

**Why.** A data error is a stored value that differs from its source, and a gap
beyond tolerance is one only where evidence says so (DE-11, since 0.17.0). A
vendor that standardizes filings leaves gaps that reproduce the filing. So DQ903
weighs four explanations, two of them filing facts and two data errors:

| Hypothesis | Evidence for it | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| `structural` | the same gap in the neighbouring periods in force (two either side, of the period type), within the larger of the tolerance and a quarter of the gap, by their median or in both periods on one side (a gap that starts or stops here: a SPAC's first quarters); the gap the series' other breaks have, as a share of its largest term (within 2.5 robust spreads of their median, with three or more); the identity failing in every neighbouring period by other amounts; or an `explained_by` item reported, non-zero, in the period | filing fact |
| `unexplained` | none of the others: a break the series' process makes now and then | filing fact, unless `unexplained_is_error` |
| `field_error` | the gap is a stored term put wrong: dropped from the total or counted twice (the term), its sign flipped (twice the term), or stored in thousands or millions where units were meant (999 or 999,999 times the term), or the other way, within a twentieth of the tolerance, with the value put right sitting with the same item in the neighbouring periods better than the stored one; or the next or last period's gap cancels it within 2% (a value in the wrong period); or the series has been clean | data error |
| `version_error` | another version of the period holds, with the same total and its gap gone (not merely inside a wider tolerance): the vendor's record corrected, or broken, in one of them. Before 0.17.0 this was `reclassification`, a filing fact | data error |

**The series' own process.** Both filing facts come at the series' own rate of
breaks for the identity and period type: its breaks in its other periods in
force, shrunk toward the cross-section's rate by `break_rate_strength` (8)
periods of it. The cross-section's rate is `[statements.break_rates]`' for the
identity, which `dorq calibrate` writes from the input's series, else
`break_rate` (5%), the rate the prior weights hold at. So a break where the
series holds in every other period is evidence of an error, and one where it
often breaks is not: the same unexplained gap is warn in the first and info in
the second.

**Chance.** A term match and a cancelling pair count against how often the
series' other breaks (of either period type, not persistent) show one by
chance, shrunk toward 2% and 1% by 20 breaks of them. Where a series' process
breaks often by gaps of the size of its terms, a match is weak evidence; where
its breaks match nothing, a match is about e^3 for an error.

`p_error` is the probability of the two data errors (and of `unexplained` with
`[statements] unexplained_is_error = true`, as before 0.17.0), through
`[statements.calibration]`'s `dq903` or `dq90` map when one is set. The severity
follows `[severity]`; a filing fact is at info. The prior weights are
`[statements.priors]`'s `identity_structural`, `identity_version_error`,
`identity_field_error` and `identity_unexplained`, relative to each other, which
`dorq calibrate` fits to labels ([calibration.md](../calibration.md#statements)).
Their defaults are e^−1.4, e^−3, e^−1.05 and e^−1.05. A field error names the
field when a term does: in the message, in `detail.field`, in `record_key` as
`item`, and in a `check_item` suggested action. Where a total wrong and a term
dropped from it both fit, the total is named, since it is wrong in either reading.

```
AAA  2010-09-30 Q3 FY2010 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 error  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 5994000000 (tolerance 50000000): the gap is total_liabilities stored at x0.001  (line 8)
CLEAN  2015-03-31 Q1 FY2015 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 warn  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 300000000 (tolerance 50000000): no term explains it, where the series holds: it breaks in 0 of 39 other quarters  (line 62)
IRREG  2015-03-31 Q1 FY2015 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 info  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 250000000 (tolerance 50000000): the series' own gap: it breaks in 19 of 39 other quarters, by a median 3.5% of its largest term  (line 63)
BBB  2010-03-31 Q1 FY2010 balance_sheet v1  DQ903 info  identity-break  balance_sheet is off by 300000000 (tolerance 50000000): as in the periods around it  (line 3)
```

The other messages are `as in the periods before it` (or `after it`), `it fails
in the periods around it too`, `net_income_noncontrolling is reported in the
period`, `another version holds, with the same total_assets`, `the next period's
gap cancels it: a value in the wrong period`, and `no term explains it, and the
series breaks in 6 of 40 other quarters`. "Stored at x0.001" is a value a
thousand times too small; "twice X: its sign" is X with its sign flipped.

**A chronic break.** An identity that fails in at least `chronic_share` (75%) of
at least `chronic_min_periods` (8) periods in force of a type is reported once
more, for the series: at warn where its median gap is at least
`chronic_warn_share` (half) of the scale (an item mapped wrong in every period),
else at info (the company's structure, as noncontrolling interests are). Each
period's own report is then a filing fact, and this one keeps the condition in
view:

```
MAPPED  2010-03-31..2019-12-31 quarter balance_sheet  DQ903 warn  identity-break  balance_sheet fails in 40 of 40 quarters in force, by a median 56% of the scale: the company's structure, or an item mapped wrong in every period  (line 4)
```

**Keys.** A report's `record_key` is the version's, with `identity`, and for a
field error also `item`:
`{"statement":"balance_sheet","period_type":"quarter","fiscal_date":"2010-09-30","version_no":1,"identity":"balance_sheet","item":"total_liabilities"}`.
A chronic report's is its statement and period type, the first period's date, and
`identity`, with no version:
`{"statement":"balance_sheet","period_type":"quarter","fiscal_date":"2010-03-31","identity":"balance_sheet"}`;
its `date` and `end_date` span the periods.

**Detail.** `identity`, `equation`, `gap` (left side minus right), `tolerance`,
`neighbours_gap` (the median of the neighbours' gaps, where there are two or
more), `share` (the gap over the largest term), `breaks` and `periods` (the
series' other periods of the type, and their breaks), `break_rate` (its rate,
shrunk), `usual_share` (its other breaks' median share, with three or more),
`cancels` (`next` or `last`) and `field`. A chronic report's: `identity`,
`equation`, `breaks`, `periods`, `median_share` and `chronic: true`. The
hypotheses and evidence are in the JSON formats' `hypotheses` and `evidence`.

**fafnir.** DQ903 succeeds `balance_sheet_identity` and `cash_flow_tie`, and
judges the subtotals they do not: current and noncurrent, gross profit, operating
income, EBIT, and the net income chain (`priors/fafnir-statements.toml`). It
does what fafnir's playbook does by hand ("a gap in every period is the company's
structure ... a gap in one period alone, unexplained by the filing, is a vendor
error"). Two things differ:

- fafnir's balance-sheet check calls a negative gap an error and never
  structural. DQ903 has no sign rule: a gap in every period is structural
  either way.
- fafnir flags every gap at one severity. DQ903 reports a filing fact at info,
  which fafnir's ingest files without a queue entry.

**SF1's identities.** The prior has the candidates of the plan's §4.2 that SF1
does not compute. `scripts/fafnir-identity-pass-rates.sql` measures each one's
pass rate on fafnir's current versions, with DQ903's tolerance. On a sample of
400 random SF1 tickers (2026-11-01, as reported and as restated):

| Identity | Pass rate | Shipped |
|---|---|---|
| `gross_profit`, `operating_income`, `ebit` | 100% | no: SF1 computes them |
| `assets_split` | 100% as reported, 99.92% restated | yes |
| `liabilities_split`, `debt_split`, `investments_split` | 99.86% to 99.995% | yes |
| `net_income`, `net_income_common` | 99.62% to 99.94% | yes |
| `cash_flow` | 96.9% to 97.0% | yes, investigated: DQ903 files 46% of its failures as structural or reclassifications |
| `balance_sheet` | 83.7% to 83.9% | yes, investigated: DQ903 files 92% of its failures as structural or reclassifications |

Two of fafnir's exemptions are balance-sheet rules DQ903 does not yet have, and
are most of what it still reports there at error: temporary equity (the parent's
equity a deficit and the gap at least 20% of assets, over too few periods to
persist: a SPAC's first year) and minority interest before SFAS 160 (a positive
gap up to 20% of assets, before 2009-12-15). DR-0912's shadow comparison
measures them against fafnir's SQL check before dorq replaces it.

## Fix

A field error: re-fetch the version, and if the vendor's value is the one named,
report it to the vendor. A structural gap: nothing to fix; add the item that
explains it to `explained_by` where the vendor reports one. A reclassification:
the newer version is the one in force. Unexplained: compare the version with the
filing.

# DQ202 scale-shift

**Family:** price action · **Severity:** by probability · **Applies to:** OHLCV
bars and point series · **Bayesian** (`p_error` = P(an error))

## What it reports

The level changes by a power of ten, or to a price no security trades at,
without the volume shift a split would cause. Either an era stored at the wrong
scale (cents as dollars, a split applied backwards) or a scale change that runs
to the end of the series. Two opposite shifts are reported once, as the era
between them. A level change by a split's ratio whose share volume stays put is
reported here too: DQ203 needs the volume to shift (doc/checks/DQ201.md).

```
LIQ13  2020-04-30..2021-02-08  DQ202 error  scale-shift  closes 2020-04-30..2021-02-08 are ×0.0009734 the level either side (×0.001): an era at the wrong scale; P(error) = >0.99
LIQ01  2022-12-09  DQ202 warn  scale-shift  close 25.54→2503 (×98 ≈ ×100); P(error) = >0.99, provisional
```

## The model

The `scale_error` hypothesis of the price model (doc/checks/DQ201.md): the move is
a power of ten (±1 to ±6, weighted toward 10 and 100) plus an ordinary return,
and volume stays where it was. Evidence for it over a split is the volume level:
a split moves share volume by its ratio, a scale error does not. A price outside
[`min_price`, `max_price`] (1e-5 to 1e6) on one side, or a change of three or
more in the decimals the closes are written with (DQ106's signal), is evidence
too.

The suggested action rescales the era. For an unpaired shift, the era is the side
whose price is implausible, or otherwise the shorter side.

A real move is not a scale shift. A value stored at the wrong scale is the old bar
times a constant: an ordinary range at the new level, on the volume the series
always trades. A news squeeze trades through a range many times the usual on
thousands of times the volume, and the volume stays up for days. Both are
evidence in the model (`bar_range`, `volume_on_day`, `volume_shift`), so a
microcap's ×7.8 squeeze on 167M shares is DQ209, not DQ202. A volume base that is
mostly zero is no evidence either way (`base_volume_zero`).

### An era before a split on file

With `--actions`, a split on file whose ratio the bars carry a few sessions
*before* its ex-date, and not on it, is either misdated (DQ701) or right, with
the sessions between stored at its post-split scale: some vendors derive their
"unadjusted" bars from adjusted ones for dates before a split they already know.
The prices are the same either way; the volume tells them apart. Sessions after a
split trade at its new level; pre-split sessions stored at the wrong scale trade
at the level before. When the levels before and from the ex-date on differ by a
factor of e or more, and the era's median volume is nearer the level before its
move than the level from the ex-date on (by half a log unit or more), the split
is taken as right and the era is reported here, with the split named and a
suggested rescale by the split's own factor. A split whose volume levels sit
closer than that (a 2:1's ×2 is within a few sessions' noise) stays DQ701:

```
10093  2026-10-05..2026-10-09  DQ202 warn  scale-shift  closes 2026-10-05..2026-10-09 are ×46.35 the close before them: the sessions before the 1:50 split on file ex 2026-10-12, stored at its post-split scale; an era at the wrong scale; P(error) = >0.99, provisional
      → re-fetch, or rescale 2026-10-05..2026-10-09 by ×0.02
```

Its `detail` adds `split_on_file` and `split_ex_date`.

## Configuration

```toml
[price]
ratio_tolerance = 0.01   # slack on a clean ratio, on the log scale
min_price = 1e-05
max_price = 1000000

[priors]
scale_error = 0.02
```

## Not reported

- A level shift with the inverse volume shift of a split (DQ203).
- A violent session, or a move on a surge of volume that stays up: a real move
  (DQ209).
- A level shift by the ratio of a split on file, whatever the volume does
  (DQ209): a feed's unadjusted volume is sometimes adjusted already.
- A shift that reverts within five bars (DQ201).

## Fix

Rescale the era by the factor in `suggested_action`, or re-fetch it.

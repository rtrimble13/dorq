# DQ107 zero-range-with-volume

**Family:** integrity · **Severity:** warn, or info on a thin series ·
**Applies to:** OHLCV bars with a volume column · **Deterministic** (`p_error` = 1)

## What it reports

A bar whose open, high, low and close are all the same, on at least the median
volume the series trades on days with any trading.

A single trade prints a flat bar, and on a thin name that is normal, so flat bars
on small volume are not reported, nor are those on less than one trade's volume.
Nor, with no ranged bar nearby, are those of a series that trades under one lot
a day, or whose price is pinned (below). A flat bar on typical-or-higher volume
is not how trading looks. More likely, the range was lost: a price stored
at a scale that rounds the range away (fafnir's `price_scale_collapse`), or a
close copied into the other three fields.

The check needs at least 20 days with trading to judge what typical volume is. On
a shorter series it reports nothing.

```
LIQ46  2020-09-04  DQ107 warn  zero-range-with-volume  open = high = low = close = 31.12 on volume 7948472 (median 4450467), where the bars nearby span a median 32.5 steps of the price grid  (line 91372)
```

## When a flat bar is ordinary

On a coarse price grid, flat bars are how trading looks. A sub-dime stock quoted
in cents moves a cent at a time, its bars span a tick or two, and a quiet day
prints one price. So DQ107 looks at the 20 bars either side of a flat one:

- **Their range, in steps of this bar's price grid.** The grid is the largest of
  three: the exchange's tick for the date and price (as the price checks use it;
  doc/checks/DQ201.md; or the `tick_size` in `--meta`), the grid most closes are written on, and the smallest gap
  between the distinct prices nearby. The last catches a lattice coarser than the
  decimals: a cent grid adjusted for a 1:9 split moves in steps of 0.0009,
  written to four decimals. When the bars nearby span a median of
  `flat_bar_steps` (3) steps or fewer, a flat bar is ordinary.
- **How often they are flat.** When at least 3 of the traded bars nearby, and at
  least one in ten, are flat among bars that do have a range, flat bars are a
  habit of that stretch.

Either way the bar is not reported. A flat bar among bars that span many steps
is, and so is a run of them: a feed that copied the close for three days is a
habit of three bars, not one in ten. A stretch where every bar is flat, with no
ranged bar nearby, is reported too ("with no bar nearby that has a range"). That
is fafnir's `price_scale_collapse`. Where such a stretch begins, the moves of the
bars before it round away at its price, so its first bars look ordinary; DQ202
reports the change of scale itself.

**A bar below one trade.** A single trade prints one price. A flat bar whose own
volume is below `[coverage] trade_size` (1,000 shares, the volume dorq takes as
one trade) is not reported, wherever it is: a 100-share day on a thin ETF whose
multi-lot days have a range.

**A pinned price.** A SPAC trading at its trust value, or a target pinned to a
deal price, prints one price a day because the price is not moving, so every bar
nearby is flat. With no ranged bar nearby, a flat bar is not reported when both
hold:

- one step of the grid is under `pinned_step_share` (0.002) of the price: the
  grid is fine enough that a moving price would show; and
- the traded closes among the same 20 bars either side move a median of
  `flat_bar_steps` (3) grid steps or fewer from one to the next.

Where the grid is coarse for the price (a sub-dime stock quoted in cents, at
14% of the price a step), still closes are what a collapsed scale looks like, and
the bar is reported. Where the closes move, flat bars are a close copied into the
other fields, and are reported too. The default of 0.002 takes a cent grid as
fine from $5: at $1.50 a cent is 0.67% of the price, and a cent or three a day is
an ordinary move, so flat bars there may be a copied close.

**A series that trades under one lot a day.** A small fund that prints one
100-share lot a day has one price a day, so every bar is flat and no bar nearby has
a range to judge by. When the series' median volume is below `[coverage]
trade_size` (1,000 shares, the volume dorq takes as one trade; doc/checks/DQ301.md),
a flat bar with no ranged bar nearby is not reported. On a series whose median is
at least that, it is still reported, and so is a flat bar among ranged ones on any
series.

## A thin series: info

On a series that trades thinly, one or two block prints make a day, and one print
has one price. fafnir's universe holds thousands of small ETFs like that; on its
first shadow nights most DQ107 reports were on them. A series is thin when its
median volume on days with trading is under `flat_bar_min_median_volume` (5,000
shares), or its median dollar volume (close × volume, on those days) is under
`flat_bar_min_median_dollar_volume` (0 by default: no dollar floor;
priors/fafnir.toml sets $500,000). Its flat bars are reported at info, with the
reason in the message:

```
17703  2026-10-13  DQ107 info  zero-range-with-volume  open = high = low = close = 91.1 on volume 3136 (median 229), ...; a thin series (a median 229 shares a day, under 5,000), where a flat bar is how trading looks
```

A flat bar on a large day stays at warn on any series: volume at least
`flat_bar_volume_multiple` (5) times the median, and itself at least the floors
(5,000 shares, and the dollar floor). Two million shares flat at one price on a
fund that trades 350,000 a day is a lost range however thin the fund is.

## Detail

`close`, `volume` and `median_volume` (the series' median on days with trading),
`median_dollar_volume`, `volume_multiple` (volume / the median) and
`thin_series`;
`grid_step`, the grid the bars nearby are judged on; `ranged_nearby`, whether
enough bars nearby have a range to judge by; `nearby_range_steps`, the median
range of those bars in steps of the grid at this bar's price, unrounded, when
they do; and `close_steps`, the median move of the closes nearby in steps, when
there are enough of them. The message rounds the steps to two decimals: a median
of 3.04 is above `flat_bar_steps` and prints as such. Each nearby range is taken
at the flat bar's price, so three cents at a slightly lower price is a little
more than three steps.

## Not reported

- Flat bars on a coarse grid, as above.
- Flat bars on less than `[coverage] trade_size`: one print.
- Flat bars with no ranged bar nearby, on a series whose median volume is below
  `[coverage] trade_size`: it trades under one lot a day.
- Flat bars with no ranged bar nearby, on a pinned price: a fine grid and closes
  that barely move.
- At warn, flat bars on a thin series (below the floors), unless the day is a
  large one: they are info.
- NAV-priced funds strike one price a day, so for them a flat bar is correct.
  Ignore DQ107 for those series with a profile:

```toml
[profiles.funds]
match = { series = ["VFIAX", "TDEAX"] }
ignore = ["DQ107"]
```

## Configuration

```toml
[integrity]
flat_bar_steps = 3   # a flat bar is ordinary where nearby bars span this many grid steps or fewer
pinned_step_share = 0.002   # with no ranged bar nearby: a grid step under this share of the price,
                            # with closes moving flat_bar_steps or fewer, is a pinned price
flat_bar_min_median_volume = 5000        # a series trading less (shares a day) is thin: info
flat_bar_min_median_dollar_volume = 0    # or less than this in dollars (0: no dollar floor)
flat_bar_volume_multiple = 5             # but a flat bar on this many times the median: warn

[coverage]
trade_size = 1000    # volume per trade: a series with a median below it trades under one lot a day
```

`trade_size` is the same setting DQ301 uses for the trades a day that volume implies,
so a change to it moves both checks; in DQ107 it sets both what one trade is and
what one lot a day is. A profile can set it for a group of series:

```toml
[profiles.odd_lots]
match = { series = ["ABCD", "EFGH"] }
coverage = { trade_size = 100 }
```

## Fix

Re-fetch the bar. If the source's range was lost to precision, the series needs a
different scale.

# DQ107 zero-range-with-volume

**Family:** integrity · **Severity:** warn · **Applies to:** OHLCV bars with a
volume column · **Deterministic** (`p_error` = 1)

## What it reports

A bar whose open, high, low and close are all the same, on at least the median
volume the series trades on days with any trading.

A single trade prints a flat bar, and on a thin name that is normal, so flat bars
on small volume are not reported. A flat bar on typical-or-higher volume is not
how trading looks. More likely, the range was lost: a price stored at a scale that
rounds the range away (fafnir's `price_scale_collapse`), or a close copied into
the other three fields.

The check needs at least 20 days with trading to judge what typical volume is. On
a shorter series it reports nothing.

```
LIQ46  2020-09-04  DQ107 warn  zero-range-with-volume  open = high = low = close = 31.12 on volume 7948472 (median 4450467), where the bars nearby span a median 32.5 steps of the price grid  (line 91372)
```

## When a flat bar is ordinary

On a coarse price grid, flat bars are how trading looks. A sub-dime stock quoted
in cents moves a cent at a time, its bars span a tick or two, and a quiet day
prints one price. So DQ107 looks at the 20 bars either side of a flat one:

- **Their range, in steps of this bar's price grid.** The grid is the largest of
  three: the exchange's tick for the date and price (as the price checks use it;
  doc/checks/DQ201.md), the grid most closes are written on, and the smallest gap
  between the distinct prices nearby. The last catches a lattice coarser than the
  decimals: a cent grid adjusted for a 1:9 split moves in steps of 0.0009,
  written to four decimals. When the bars nearby span a median of
  `flat_bar_steps` (3) steps or fewer, a flat bar is ordinary.
- **How often they are flat.** When at least 3 of the traded bars nearby, and at
  least one in ten, are flat among bars that do have a range, flat bars are a
  habit of that stretch.

Either way the bar is not reported. A flat bar among bars that span many steps
is, and so is a run of them: a feed that copied the close for three days is a
habit of three bars, not one in ten. A stretch where every bar is flat, with no
ranged bar nearby, is reported too ("with no bar nearby that has a range"). That
is fafnir's `price_scale_collapse`. Where such a stretch begins, the moves of the
bars before it round away at its price, so its first bars look ordinary; DQ202
reports the change of scale itself.

## Not reported

- Flat bars on a coarse grid, as above.
- NAV-priced funds strike one price a day, so for them a flat bar is correct.
  Ignore DQ107 for those series with a profile:

```toml
[profiles.funds]
match = { series = ["VFIAX", "TDEAX"] }
ignore = ["DQ107"]
```

## Configuration

```toml
[integrity]
flat_bar_steps = 3   # a flat bar is ordinary where nearby bars span this many grid steps or fewer
```

## Fix

Re-fetch the bar. If the source's range was lost to precision, the series needs a
different scale.

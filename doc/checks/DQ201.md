# DQ201 bad-print

**Family:** price action · **Severity:** by probability · **Applies to:** OHLCV
bars and point series · **Bayesian** (`p_error` = P(the bar is wrong))

## What it reports

A bar, or a block of up to `revert_max_bars` (5) bars, that is off the series and
then comes back: a spike and a reversion. A decimal slip (×10, ×0.01), a value
from the wrong day, or a stray print. The violation spans the block, and the
suggested action is to delete or re-fetch it.

```
LIQ01  2019-06-26  DQ201 error  bad-print  close 341.36 between 151.4 and 150.2 (×2.255, then back on the next bar); P(error) = >0.99
LIQ10  2019-10-18..2019-10-21  DQ201 error  bad-print  2 bars at ×1.983 the level either side (close 32.39→64.22, back to 33.07); P(error) = >0.99
```

This page also describes the model that DQ202–DQ205 and DQ209 share.

## The model

The question is not "is this move unusual?" but "which explanation fits it
best?" One model scores every suspicious bar, and each DQ2xx check reports the
bars whose most probable *error* is its own. A move that is probably real becomes
DQ209 (info).

### Features (computed once per series)

- **Bars.** Rows with a finite close above zero, the first row of each date.
  *Carry bars* — no volume, the last close repeated — are left out: they record
  that nothing traded, not a price, so the next trade's move spans all the
  sessions since the last real one.
- **Returns** on the log scale, `r = log(close / previous close)`, with the
  number of sessions between the two bars (from the calendar).
- **Volatility** per session from a discounted Normal-Inverse-Gamma posterior
  (forgetting factor `volatility_discount`, 0.97: about 33 returns), run forward
  and backward over returns scaled by √sessions and clipped at 4 standard
  deviations, so one spike cannot swamp it. A bar is judged against the
  volatility either side of it, leaving the bar and the few after it out.
- **A prior on volatility** for the start of the series: a class default by
  price level and dollar volume (8% a day under $1 down to 2% above $20, ×1.5
  under $100k a day), combined with the series' own robust scale (the MAD of
  its returns), weighted as ten returns.
- **The price grid**: the exchange's tick for the date and price (⅛ until June
  1997, 1/16 until April 2001, then a cent, or 0.0001 under $1), or the coarser
  grid most prices are written on — a sub-dime stock quoted in cents moves a cent
  at a time. Every ordinary move includes a bid-ask bounce of 0.7 grid steps, so
  a one-tick move on a 2-cent stock is ordinary. A `tick_size` in `--meta`
  replaces the exchange's tick.
- **With `--market`**, returns are taken net of the market's move times a rolling
  beta, and an ordinary move widens by half the market's move on a big market
  day (doc/context.md, DQ602).

### Screening

A bar is scored when its return is improbable as ordinary (two-sided tail below
`candidate_tail_prob`, 0.001), or it moves 50% or more either way
(`floor_move`, so dorq never misses what fafnir's old outlier check caught), or
it follows a gap of `segment_gap` sessions (DQ205), or its close lies outside a
range that holds its open (DQ204).

### Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Story | Prior |
|---|---|---|
| `market_move` | A real move: Student t with `tail_dof` (4) degrees of freedom, with a `jump_prob` (3%) chance of a jump `jump_scale` (6) times larger | 0.90 |
| `tick_move` | One or two steps of the price grid | 0.01 |
| `bad_print` | Bars t..t+k−1 are off by a factor and the series then reverts (k ≤ 5, each block length half as likely as the last) | 0.05 |
| `bad_close` | Only the close field is wrong; open, high and low held the level (DQ204) | 0.01 |
| `unreported_split` | The level shifts by a split ratio and volume shifts inversely (DQ203) | 0.02 |
| `scale_error` | The level shifts by a power of ten, or to an implausible price (DQ202) | 0.02 |
| `history_segment` | After a long gap, a different security's history continues (DQ205) | 0.05 |
| `explained_split` | *With `--actions`*: a split on file between this bar and the one before; the move is its ratio plus an ordinary move. Not an error: DQ209 | 0.9 |
| `explained_dividend` | *With `--actions`*: cash dividends on file between this bar and the one before came off the price; the move net of them is an ordinary move. Not an error: DQ209 | 0.9 |

Priors are per scored bar and normalized over the hypotheses that apply: no
`bad_close` without open, high and low; no `unreported_split` for point series;
no `history_segment` without a gap; no `tick_move` beyond 2.5 grid steps; no
`explained_split` without a split on file whose ex-date falls after the bar
before and on or before this one (between two stored bars, if the ex-date has
none); no `explained_dividend` without a dividend on file there that moves the
price more than an ordinary move does.

A split on file is documentary evidence, so `explained_split` does not ask the
volume to confirm it. Its return term is as strict as `unreported_split`'s: the
split's ratio plus an ordinary move. Its bar's own fields, its volume on the day
and the plausibility gate are `unreported_split`'s too, so a price level that is
implausible on one side gives it no cover. But the bars after it are a real
move's (a reverse split is often followed by a selloff), and its volume level
after may land anywhere from unchanged to the split's ratio: a feed's
"unadjusted" volume is sometimes adjusted for the split already, and a reverse
split draws a burst of trading. Without a split on file, the inverse volume shift
is what tells `unreported_split` from `scale_error`, and it still does.

`explained_dividend` sums the cash dividends on file with ex-dates after the bar
before and on or before this one. An amount DQ705 reports at error (zero or
less, or at or above the close before) is not credited; one it reports at warn
(a special dividend ten times the usual) is. A dividend that moves the price
less than an ordinary move does explains nothing a real move does not, and
weighing it would only dilute the errors' share of the prior on every ex-date,
so it is left out. The hypothesis is a real move net of the dividend,
`log(close / (previous close − dividend))`, in every term: a distribution moves
no volume and stirs up no volatility. When the price did not fall, the move left
is the dividend itself, so a wrong row on file invents no move: `market_move` on
the raw return still wins.

### Evidence

Each hypothesis's likelihood is a product of terms. Each term's log Bayes
factor, for the reported hypothesis against `market_move`, is in the output
(`--show-evidence`, or `evidence` in JSON):

- **return**: the move itself. Under an error its size is broad (a Cauchy on the
  log scale, scale 0.3); under a split or a scale error it is the clean ratio
  plus an ordinary return.
- **next_bars**: the next five returns. A bad print's block ends in a move that
  undoes it, so the move from before the block to after it is ordinary. A real
  move, or a split on file, stirs volatility up (half the time, by a quarter of
  its size); an unreported split, a scale error or a bad print leaves the bars
  after it calm.
- **close_in_range**, **open_high_low**: the bar's own fields. A close outside a
  range that holds the open blames the close; open, high and low at the old
  level point to `bad_close`, at the new level to everything else.
- **volume_on_day**: a real move's volume rises with how surprising the move is;
  a bad print's does not.
- **volume_shift**: the median volume over `volume_window` (40) bars after
  against before. An unreported split moves share volume by its ratio; a split
  on file anywhere from not at all to its ratio; a real move keeps some dollar
  volume; an error leaves it alone.
- **plausible_level**: a price outside [`min_price`, `max_price`] is evidence of
  an error at the side that is implausible.
- **precision_shift**: the decimals the closes are written with changing by
  three or more at the boundary (DQ106's signal) points to a scale error.
- **listing_price**, **volatility_shift**: after a long gap, a first close at
  10, 20, 25, 50 or 100, or a new volatility regime, point to a new history.

`p_error` is the posterior probability of the error hypotheses together, and maps
to a severity through `[severity]` (info ≥ 0.2, warn ≥ 0.6, error ≥ 0.9). The
code is the most probable error hypothesis's. Below info, the bar is DQ209.

### Point series

A point series (one value per date: a rate, a spread, an index level) goes
through the same model on the scale its `transform` sets (`[price] transform`):

- **`log`**: as for prices; moves are ratios. Every value must be positive.
- **`diff`**: moves are changes, `y = value`. This is for rates and spreads that
  sit near zero or cross it, where a quarter-point move is a quarter point
  whatever the level.
- **`auto`** (the default): `log` when every value is positive, else `diff`.

On the `diff` scale, a wrong value's jump is in proportion to the series'
typical magnitude: 0.3 × the larger of its median absolute value and 20 times its
volatility. The volatility prior is the series' own robust scale; the grid is the
decimals the values are written to. A scale error multiplies rather than adds.
A value stored k times too large has density `f(v / k − previous) / k`, and the
first value back has `f(v − previous / k)`. Only ratios of 100 or more count:
percent as a decimal, thousands as millions. The size of the moves either side
is evidence too. Values stored ×100 move ×100 as much. A rate that falls a
quarter point to zero lands near a hundredth of where it was, but its moves stay
the size they were, so it is not a change of units.

With `bounds` set (DQ108), a value outside them is implausible as a real move,
just as a price outside [`min_price`, `max_price`] is.

```
RAT02  2021-02-26  DQ201 error  bad-print  value 470 between 4.7 and 4.69 (+465.3, then back on the next bar); P(error) = >0.99
```

### The newest bars

With fewer than `provisional_bars` (3) bars after it, whether a move reverts is
not yet known. The violation is marked `provisional`, and its severity is at
most warn; a later run decides. A bad newest bar and a scale change from the
newest bar look alike until then, so the code may be DQ202 on the first night.

## Configuration

```toml
[price]
candidate_tail_prob = 0.001
floor_move = 0.5
revert_max_bars = 5
volume_window = 40
provisional_bars = 3
volatility_discount = 0.97
tail_dof = 4
jump_prob = 0.03
jump_scale = 6
min_price = 1e-05
max_price = 1000000
transform = "auto"   # point series: "log", "diff" or "auto"

[priors]
market_move = 0.9
bad_print = 0.05
```

Profiles may set `price` and `priors` per series (`[profiles.<name>.price]`).

## Not reported

- A move followed by the volatility a real move brings, or on the heavy volume
  real news brings.
- One- and two-tick moves on a coarse grid.
- The move back from a reported bad print: it is part of the same violation.

## Fix

Delete the bars or re-fetch them from the source. A newest bar marked
provisional is best left for the next run.

# DQ921 share-terms-step

**Family:** statements, share counts (DQ92x) · **Severity:** warn by default, from
`p_error` · **Applies to:** statements, with `--actions` and `--items` · **Bayesian**
(the share terms model, [statements.md](../statements.md#share-counts))

## What it reports

A share count that steps by a clean split ratio or a power of ten from one period
to the next, once the counts are in one share terms. A count is stored in the terms
of its version's `known_from` (fafnir's ADR 0018). dorq puts each count into the
latest terms with the splits on file. After that, a count moves only by issuance
and buybacks. A step that is still left is one of:

| Hypothesis | What it means | Classification | Suggested action |
|---|---|---|---|
| `real_issuance` | an offering, a merger or a buyback | not reported | - |
| `unreported_split` | a split missing from the actions, its ex-date between the two counts' knowledge dates | `context_gap` | `add_split`, with `ratio`, `ex_date_after` and `ex_date_on_or_before` |
| `terms_error` | a count stored in the terms before a split on file in the window, or after it | `data_error` | `check_share_terms`, with `item`, `fiscal_date` and `split` |
| `unit_error` | a count stored at ×1,000, ×0.001, ×10⁶ or ×10⁻⁶ | `data_error` | `check_share_terms` |

`p_error` is the chance that the step is not the company's own (1 −
`real_issuance`). The evidence weighs:

- **The step.** The step is taken net of the count's own trend, such as a steady
  buyback, and against the spread of its real steps. A split's step is its ratio
  plus that real change.
- **The weighted average's place in its period** (`weighted_in_period`). A
  period's weighted average lies between its outstanding counts at the start and
  the end. An issuance puts it anywhere between them. A split restates the whole
  period, and so does a filing stored in other terms: the average then sits at the
  period's end, and the outstanding count steps with it.
- **The market cap** (`market_cap_step`). This is the count as stored times the
  close at its knowledge date, both in that date's terms. A missing split leaves it
  level. An issuance at the market price steps it, and so does a count stored
  wrong. Its spread is the line's own, with fat tails.
- **The price** (`close_before`). Companies split forward at high prices and
  reverse-split at low ones.
- **The window.** A missing split's prior grows with its window: a restatement
  filed years later spans more ex-dates than a quarter does. A terms error needs a
  split on file in the window, at its ratio or twice it.

```
IND05  2021-06-30 Q4 FY2021 income v1  DQ921 error  share-terms-step  weighted_average_shares steps by 2:1 from 2021-03-31 to 2021-06-30, with no split on file: a 2:1 split missing from the actions, its ex-date after 2021-05-06 and on or before 2021-08-09; it explains 7 other steps in the counts  (line 1467)
IND01  2018-12-31 Q4 FY2018 income v1  DQ921 error  share-terms-step  weighted_average_shares steps by 1:2 from 2018-09-30 to 2018-12-31, against the split on file (2:1 2018-12-06): a count stored in the wrong share terms  (line 268)
IND21  2016-09-30..2016-12-31 Q4 FY2016 income v1  DQ921 error  share-terms-step  weighted_average_shares steps by x0.001 from 2016-06-30 to 2016-09-30: a count stored at a power of ten, to 2016-12-31  (line 6520)
```

**One missing split, one report.** A missing split leaves a step wherever a count
known before it meets one known after it. That happens at the period it came in,
and again wherever a version filed after it (a restatement in the new terms) meets
one filed before. Each missing split is reported once, on its narrowest step. The
others it explains fold into that report: DQ921's other steps, items and period
types, and DQ922's mismatches. Its window is narrowed to theirs in common
(`steps_explained`).

**A run.** A count stored wrong for several periods steps in and back out. Its
factor is measured on both steps, and it is reported once, on the first period,
with `end_date`. A missing split shifts the level for good, so a step back is
never its end.

**Keys.** The version's `record_key`, with `item`. The detail holds `item`,
`step` (after / before, in the latest terms), `ratio`, `previous_fiscal_date`,
`window_from` and `window_to` (the knowledge dates), `market_cap_step` with close,
and `views`.

**Versions.** Both views are judged, in force and as first reported. A finding
both views share is one report.

**Without `--actions`** there are no splits to put the counts into one terms, and
DQ921 judges nothing. Without `--items`, which names the share counts
(`item_class` `share_count`), neither does it.

## Fix

- A missing split: add it to the actions (fafnir's `core.corporate_action`), its
  ex-date within the window. The bars' DQ203 often has the date.
- A count stored wrong: re-fetch the period from the filing named in the suggested
  action, and report the vendor's value to the vendor.

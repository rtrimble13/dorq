# DQ932 restatement-implausible

**Family:** statements, the knowledge timeline (DQ93x) · **Severity:** warn by
default, from `p_error` · **Applies to:** statements · **Bayesian**
([statements.md](../statements.md#the-knowledge-timeline))

## What it reports

A new version of a period that differs from the one before it as no restatement
does. Either the new version is wrong, or the old one was. Each pair of a period's
consecutive versions, in the order they became known, is weighed:

| Hypothesis | What it means | Reported on |
|---|---|---|
| `restatement` | a filing fact: a few components moved, totals kept, or values moved by a few per cent | not reported |
| `new_version_error` | the new version is stored wrong | the new version |
| `old_version_error` | the old version was stored wrong, and the new one corrects it. Every point-in-time read in its interval saw it | the old version |

The money items are compared: all of them without `--items`, and with it the flows
and stocks. Share counts and per-share values move with splits. The evidence:

- **The pattern** of the changed items:

  | Pattern | What it is | Under a restatement | Under an error |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `power_of_ten` | four in five of them moved by one power of ten | 1e-4 | 0.3 |
  | `sign` | four in five of them negated | 0.001 | 0.15 |
  | `swap` | two values changed places | 0.001 | 0.1 |
  | `total_only` | an identity's total moved while its terms held | 0.01 | 0.14 |
  | `offset` | an amount moved from one item to another: a reclassification | 0.5 | 0.01 |
  | `none` | anything else | 0.49 | 0.3 |

- **The periods around it.** Each version's values are judged against what the two
  periods either side predict, in the spread of the item's own residuals along its
  line. The old version is judged against the periods as first reported, the ones
  in force beside it, and the new one against the periods in force. A restatement
  that moves a whole fiscal year fits both. A version stored wrong fits neither its
  own neighbours nor the other's. The fit is taken over most of the changed items
  (the lower median), so an amount moved to an item that was 0 does not decide it.
- **Priors.** Each of a new version and an old one is wrong 0.01 of the time.

`p_error` is `new_version_error` plus `old_version_error`.

```
IND10  2008-06-30 Q2 FY2008 cash_flow v2  DQ932 error  restatement-implausible  v2, known from 2026-10-20, differs from v1 by x1000 on 10 of 10 items, against the periods around it: the new version is wrong  (line 3002)
IND07  2011-09-30 Q4 FY2011 cash_flow v1  DQ932 warn  restatement-implausible  v1, in force from 2011-11-17 to 2026-10-20, differs from the restated v2 by x0.001 on 9 of 9 items; v2 agrees with the periods around it and v1 does not: the old version was wrong while in force  (line 2033)
```

**Keys.** The `record_key` of the version reported, the new one or the old. The
detail holds `pattern`, `items_changed`, `power` (new = old × 10^power), `old_version`,
`new_version`, `old_off_neighbours` and `new_off_neighbours` (in spreads), and for an
old version `in_force_from` and `in_force_to`.

**Cohorts.** A DQ932 report on a row known the day DQ951 finds a load fault (many
series carrying DQ932 that day) goes to info, linked by `cohort_date`.

## Fix

- A new version wrong: re-fetch it from its filing, and report it to the vendor.
- An old version wrong: the data in force now is right. Every backtest or report
  that read the period between `in_force_from` and `in_force_to` saw the wrong
  figures, so rerun what depends on them.

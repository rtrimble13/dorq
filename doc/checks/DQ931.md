# DQ931 filing-date-implausible

**Family:** statements, the knowledge timeline (DQ93x) · **Severity:** warn by
default, from `p_error`: error for a date too early, warn at most for an amendment's
date, info for a late filing · **Applies to:** statements · **Bayesian**
([statements.md](../statements.md#the-knowledge-timeline))

## What it reports

A filing date that the issuer's own filings and the SEC's deadlines say is wrong.
A date too early lets every backtest know the figures too soon: the look-ahead
fafnir's constraints exist to prevent.

DQ931 judges each period's first version that was filed (`knowledge_source`
`filed`). A version the loader estimated or observed has no filing date. Its lag is
`known_from` less the period end. The income statement and balance sheet of one
period, known from one date, are one filing, reported once. A year's last quarter
and the year itself are known from the 10-K; the other quarters from a 10-Q.

| Hypothesis | Evidence that favours it | Reported |
|---|---|---|
| `ordinary` | the lag sits with the issuer's other lags of the same form | no |
| `late_filing` | one long lag, then the issuer's usual lag again: a filing fact | info, `market_fact` |
| `misdated_early` | below any filer's lag (7 days), or far below the issuer's own: the period end or a press release's date | up to error |
| `misdated_amendment` | a long lag, and the period known after the next one: an amendment's date | up to warn |

- **The issuer's usual lag.** The median and spread of its nearest 12 filings of
  the same form on each side, the filing itself left out (a spread of 3 days at
  least). With fewer than 4, the SEC's deadlines stand in: 42 days for a 10-Q and 75
  for a 10-K, give or take 15.
- **The successor.** A period known after the next period of its type is likely
  for an amendment's date (0.9), rare for a late filing (0.02), and next to never
  otherwise.
- **Priors.** A filing date is too early 0.002 of the time, an amendment's 0.002,
  and late 0.01: `[statements.priors]`'s `filing_early`, `filing_amendment` and
  `filing_late`, which `dorq calibrate` fits to labels
  ([calibration.md](../calibration.md#statements)).

`p_error` is `misdated_early` plus `misdated_amendment`, through
`[statements.calibration]`'s `dq93` map when one is set. A date known before the
period ends is DQ901's, not DQ931's.

```
IND30  2013-12-31 Q2 FY2014 income v1  DQ931 error  filing-date-implausible  known from 2013-12-31, 0 days after the period ends, against this issuer's usual 35 days (10-Q): too early for a filing (the period end or a press release's date), so every backtest knows the figures too soon  (line 9125)
IND04  2016-09-30 Q3 FY2016 income v1  DQ931 warn  filing-date-implausible  known from 2017-04-12, 194 days after the period ends, against this issuer's usual 33 days (10-Q): an amendment's date taken for the original's, known after the next period (2016-12-31, known from 2017-03-08)  (line 1111)
IND05  2017-12-31 Q2 FY2018 income v1  DQ931 info  filing-date-implausible  known from 2018-03-14, 73 days after the period ends, against this issuer's usual 32 days (10-Q): a late filing, a filing fact  (line 1429)
```

**Keys.** The version's `record_key`, on the income statement where the filing has
one. The detail holds `lag_days`, `usual_lag_days`, `usual_lag_sd`, `form`,
`known_from`, and `next_known_from` where the period has a successor.

## Fix

Look the filing up in EDGAR and correct the version's `known_from` and
`filing_date` (fafnir's `core.*_statement`). A date too early is the urgent one: a
backtest that read the period before its real filing date has look-ahead bias.

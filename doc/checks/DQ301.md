# DQ301 missing-run

**Family:** coverage · **Severity:** by probability · **Applies to:** daily
series · **Bayesian** (`p_error` = P(feed outage))

## What it reports

A run of sessions with no bar that looks like a feed outage, judged against how
often *this* series normally has a bar. A missing day on a name that trades
millions of shares is a problem. A missing day on a name that trades on a third of
its sessions is not. A 90-session hole in either is. The check replaces fafnir's
`gap` (one flag per missing session for dense series) and its fixed 80% density
cutoff.

```
L25  2023-03-14  DQ301 warn  missing-run  1 session with no bar; nearby the series has a bar on 99.2% of sessions and trades a median 1,014,993 a day; P(feed outage) = 0.83
T0  2023-07-27..2023-12-11  DQ301 error  missing-run  96 sessions with no bar; nearby the series has a bar on 13.9% of sessions; P(feed outage) = >0.99
```

## The model

The model looks at the sessions between the series' first and last bar that fall
on a session. Each missing run (consecutive sessions with no bar) is judged by a
two-state hidden Markov model:

- **Healthy feed.** A session has no bar with probability 1 − *d*, where *d* is
  the series' density near the run. The run's own sessions, and other nearby
  sessions already judged to be outages, are left out of *d*. The model iterates
  that judgement, starting from the density with every missing session counted
  as healthy (and the volume floor below).
- **Outage.** Every session is missing. An outage starts on a given session with
  probability `outage_start` (default 1e-4) and ends with probability
  `outage_end` (default 0.05; mean length 20 sessions).

"Near the run" means the run's block of `block_sessions` (60) sessions and the
blocks either side. Counts alone can never say that a liquid name misses fewer
than about one session in *n*, and a real gap in such a name is far rarer than
that. So when the bars carry volume and the counts already show a bar on 90% or
more of the sessions nearby, the healthy density is at least what the volume
implies. Below 90%, trades are lumpy — one 2,000-share print is not two trades —
and the counts are the better guide. A median of *V* a day is roughly *V* / `trade_size` (default
1,000) trades, and a session with none then has probability e^(−*V*/`trade_size`).
That is nil for a name trading millions of shares, and no claim at all for one
trading a few. The floor for even the most liquid name is 1e-6, because an
all-day halt is rare but not impossible.

The posterior P(outage) for each session comes from the forward-backward
algorithm. The run's `p_error` is the largest of them, and maps to a severity
through `[severity]` (info ≥ 0.2, warn ≥ 0.6, error ≥ 0.9).

When many series miss the same session, DQ303 reports it once as a failed load.
A member whose run is only cohort dates has its DQ301 downgraded to info, and its
message names the cohort. A longer run that merely contains a cohort date is its
own outage and is reported as usual.

## Configuration

```toml
[coverage]
report = "run"         # or "session": one violation per missing session, like fafnir's `gap`
outage_start = 0.0001
outage_end = 0.05
block_sessions = 60
trade_size = 1000
prior_density = 0.999  # a weak prior on the density the counts estimate
prior_strength = 2
frequency = "auto"     # DQ3xx coverage runs on daily series only
```

## Not reported

- Weekly and lower-frequency series. DQ305 covers those.
- Before a series' first bar.
- After its last bar. DQ304 judges a series that stops.
- A series whose market keeps a different calendar, such as a bond-market rate
  under XNYS, shows that market's holidays as one-session gaps. Without volume
  data these stay well below the reporting threshold. To silence them entirely,
  give such series a fitting calendar with `--calendar-file`.

## Fix

Re-fetch the window from the source. If the source has no bars for it either, the
data does not exist there; accept the gap.

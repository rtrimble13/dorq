# DQ601 cohort-move

**Family:** cross-sectional · **Severity:** warn · **Applies to:** OHLCV bars,
across the series of a run

## What it reports

Several series (`cohort_min_series`, 3, or more) whose closes move by the same
clean split ratio on the same date, with no split on file to explain them. There
are two possibilities:

- **A family splitting together.** When every series is in the same `peer_group`
  (`--meta`), the funds split on the same day and the vendor reported none of it.
  This is classified as a context gap.
- **A vendor's mass adjustment.** When the series are unrelated, a feed may have
  adjusted many histories at once, or the actions file may be missing a batch of
  splits. This is classified as a data error.

```
(all series)  2020-01-23  DQ601 warn  cohort-move  4 series move ×0.4901 (≈ a 2:1 split) on 2020-01-23: LIQ27, LIQ32, LIQ48, LIQ52; all of peer group FAM1: a family splitting together, with no split on file
```

The plan's example is iShares' JKD, JKG and JKI on 2020-05-04.

The message names the first six series. `detail.members` lists every one, by
id, separated by commas (`"LIQ27,LIQ32,LIQ48,LIQ52"`), with `series_moving`
their count, `ratio` the split ratio and `factor` the median move.

## Sibling evidence

Within a peer group, a sibling that moves by the same ratio on the same day is
strong evidence that a series split. Families split together, and an unrelated
move that matches a sibling's ratio to the day is rare. Once every series is in,
each such move's posterior is updated with a Bayes factor of 100 for
`unreported_split`. If that makes the split its most probable error, the move is
reported as DQ203, replacing what the series reported for that bar. Its evidence
then shows a `peer_group` term:

```
LIQ27  2020-01-23  DQ203 error  unreported-split  close 69.6→37.44 (×0.5379 ≈ a 2:1 split), volume ×1.628 after; P(error) = >0.99
        peer_group 3 (3 in FAM1 move by the same ratio that day)  +4.6
```

A move counts when the price model scored it and its factor is within 10% of a
split ratio. Moves a split or a cash dividend on file explains, a NAV fund's
pending distribution, moves that DQ7xx reports, and the newest bars
(provisional) do not count. A fund family's year-end distributions, on file, are
not a family splitting together.

## Unrelated series: the day's base rate

Chance alone moves many series near a split ratio on any day. On fafnir's
universe of about 15,800 series, 29 to 200 a day land within 7% of ×0.8 (5:4),
12 to 45 within 7% of ×1.25, and 0 to 2 within 7% of ×0.5. Three or five
unrelated small caps that each moved 1–10% off a ratio on news are no cohort. So
when the series are not one family:

1. **Each member's move must be clean.** It counts when it is within its
   tolerance of the ratio: twice `hypot(ratio_tolerance, its ordinary move's sd)`,
   the slack the price model's split hypothesis allows, at most 10%. A liquid
   name's ×0.5302 is 6% off 2:1, well outside it.
2. **A member that is a real move by its own evidence is dropped:** a violent
   session or a surge of volume that stays up (doc/checks/DQ201.md). They are
   listed in `detail.members_dropped_as_market`.
3. **The count must beat the day's rate.** Every one-session move of every daily
   series in the input is counted in each ratio's band (within the series' own
   tolerance) and beside it (out to three times the tolerance). The base rate for
   a date is the band's mean count over the input's ten dates either side, or half
   the count beside the band that day (a day the whole market moves fills both),
   whichever is larger. The cohort is reported only when its count is improbable
   under that rate: a Poisson upper tail at or below `cohort_move_max_tail`
   (`[coverage]`, 1e-4). `detail.base_rate` and `detail.p_value` give both.

A family (every member in one `peer_group`) is judged as before: siblings
splitting together are evidence in themselves. Fifty unrelated series moving
×0.5 on one date with inverse volume, against a base rate of none, are still a
cohort.

## Record key

`record_key` adds the cohort's `ratio`: `{"trade_date": "2026-10-06", "ratio":
"2:1"}`. One date can hold a 2:1 cohort and a 3:2 one; fafnir's
one-flag-per-condition dedupe sees two conditions.

## Not reported

- Without metadata, sibling evidence needs a `peer_group`; a cohort of three or
  more unrelated series is still reported, if it beats the day's base rate.
- Unrelated series whose count chance explains, members whose move is not clean,
  and members that are real moves by their own evidence.
- Results are held until every series is in, as for DQ303.

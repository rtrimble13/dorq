# DQ106 precision-shift

**Family:** integrity · **Severity:** warn · **Applies to:** all series ·
**Deterministic** (`p_error` = 1)

## What it reports

A point where the precision closes are written with changes regime. Quoted prices
need few decimal places, such as 12.34 or 0.0125. A computed price, such as one
the vendor has back-adjusted for a split or dividend, needs as many places as the
storage holds, such as 12.345678.

A feed that should be raw but has an era of computed prices has been adjusted
somewhere upstream. This is how fafnir found WZRD and SMUP, whose "unadjusted"
histories had later reverse splits applied.

Precision is measured on the close as written, **ignoring trailing zeros**. An
export that pads every value to six places (`10.500000`) still reads as one
decimal place. A close counts as *high precision* when it needs at least
`precision_high_decimals` decimal places **and** `precision_high_sig_figs`
significant figures. The second condition keeps quoted sub-penny prices such as
0.000123 out.

dorq finds the single split point that best separates the two regimes. It reports
that point when both sides have at least `precision_min_segment` closes and the
share of high-precision closes differs by at least `precision_min_contrast`. A
series has at most one violation.

```
WZRD  2023-11-20  DQ106 warn  precision-shift  close precision changes here: 97% of 640 earlier closes need 5+ decimals, 0% of 210 later ones do; the earlier prices look computed (a back-adjusted history?) rather than quoted
```

## Configuration

```toml
[integrity]
precision_high_decimals = 5
precision_high_sig_figs = 5
precision_min_segment = 20
precision_min_contrast = 0.8
```

## Fix

Confirm the scale against an outside source. Then rescale the computed era back to
as-traded prices, or re-fetch it from a feed that is raw.
